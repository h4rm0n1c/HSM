# HSM Deposition Protocol

This document defines a deposition-style interview protocol for extracting human-state information relevant to the Human State Machine project.

It is designed for use by either humans or language models.

It is not a legal deposition template, therapy protocol, clinical assessment, or forensic instrument. It is an HSM engineering tool for structured testimony, state extraction, uncertainty preservation, and runtime-packet design.

## Summary

The goal is not to collect a tidy life story.

The goal is to expose the machinery of a human subject:

```text
what changes them
what stabilises them
what they do before they can explain themselves
what they later claim was happening
what evidence supports or contradicts those claims
what uncertainty must remain visible
```

A language model can model human language, narrative, and visible behavioural patterns. It does not possess a body, a continuous vulnerable life, fatigue, shame, pain, sensory overload, social stakes, or lived consequences.

Because of that, HSM depositions must ask about the things language models tend to fake or over-smooth:

- body state before language
- involuntary trigger responses
- anchors and stabilisers
- action gaps and executive friction
- private stakes
- social power maps
- contradiction
- absence and non-events
- tacit skill
- memory activation
- self-deception
- falsification
- compression priority

The output should not be a personality profile.

The output should be structured material that can feed:

- evidence records
- claim records
- quiescent state
- affective patterns
- anchor records
- embodied-state fields
- executive-friction fields
- social/stakes context
- runtime packets
- integrity checks
- open uncertainties

## Core principle

Do not ask only what the subject thinks.

Ask:

```text
what happened
what the body did
what the subject did
what the subject thought later
what evidence exists
what remains uncertain
what would falsify the explanation
```

The subject's later explanation may be important. It is not automatically the cause.

A later explanation may be accurate, incomplete, defensive, confabulated, socially shaped, or the higher-reasoning layer catching up after a faster state change.

## Why a language model needs this

Language models are strong at producing human-shaped explanations.

That is useful and dangerous.

A model may:

- accept a tidy story too easily
- infer motive from prose style
- underweight bodily load
- miss sensory distress
- miss authority pressure
- treat absence as non-evidence
- smooth contradiction into a single trait
- mistake later explanation for original cause
- mistake emotional coherence for evidence
- assume knowing what to do means being able to do it

This protocol compensates by forcing explicit state questions and evidence classification.

## Anti-agreement discipline

Apply the anti-agreement harness throughout.

Do not optimize for agreement, reassurance, or theory-preservation.

For each significant claim, classify it when useful:

```text
supported
plausible_but_unproven
useful_design_fiction
emotionally_coherent_but_evidentially_weak
contradicted_by_current_material
unknown
needs_source_check
needs_schema_or_test_before_durable
```

Separate:

```text
Evidence:
What has been checked or can be pointed to.

Inference:
What appears to follow from the evidence.

Uncertainty:
What has not been checked, is unavailable, or remains ambiguous.

Risk:
Where the interviewer or model may be over-smoothing, agreeing, hallucinating, or preserving the theory.

Next useful move:
The concrete inspection, schema, test, record, or rejection step.
```

## Consent, scope, and safety

Before using this protocol with a person, establish scope.

Minimum preflight:

1. Explain what the protocol is for.
2. Explain what it is not for.
3. Identify who will read the answers.
4. Identify where records will be stored.
5. Let the subject skip questions.
6. Let the subject pause or stop.
7. Mark sensitive sections.
8. Do not push for traumatic detail when structural description is enough.
9. Do not treat distress as proof.
10. Do not treat refusal as absence of relevant state.

Useful opening statement:

```text
This is not about forcing a perfect life story.
It is about mapping state transitions, evidence, uncertainty, and failure modes.
You can skip, pause, qualify, or mark anything as uncertain.
Contradictions are not failure. They are signal.
```

For legal, medical, clinical, or safeguarding contexts, use qualified professionals and appropriate procedures. HSM records may contain sensitive personal material and should be handled accordingly.

## Roles

### Human interviewer

The human interviewer should:

- ask one cluster at a time
- avoid leading the subject into a theory
- capture exact phrases when important
- ask how the subject knows
- ask what would contradict the answer
- avoid turning the answer into a diagnosis
- preserve uncertainty

### Language-model interviewer

The language-model interviewer should:

- state uncertainty plainly
- classify claims
- avoid agreement drift
- ask for evidence type
- separate reaction from explanation
- identify possible overreach
- summarize in structured records
- not pretend to be a therapist, clinician, lawyer, or person

### Subject

The subject should be invited to provide:

- direct memories
- body-state descriptions
- later explanations
- examples
- contradictions
- records that may support or contradict claims
- uncertainty and corrections

The subject is allowed to say:

```text
I do not know.
I only know this later.
That is a guess.
That feels true but I cannot prove it.
I have records for that.
Someone else saw it differently.
I do not want to answer that.
```

## Process flow

### Phase 0: Preflight

Goal: set boundaries and prevent false authority.

Steps:

1. Identify subject and purpose.
2. Define storage and visibility.
3. Define stop/skip rules.
4. Define sensitivity labels.
5. Establish claim classification language.
6. Confirm this is not clinical/legal truth by default.

Output:

- scope note
- consent note
- sensitivity rules
- known constraints

### Phase 1: Baseline and quiescent state

Goal: identify what the subject returns to when not actively disturbed.

Output:

- baseline state notes
- stable-day pattern
- unstable-day early markers
- default threat/energy profile
- natural drift activities

### Phase 2: State transition mapping

Goal: identify what changes the subject.

Output:

- trigger candidates
- bodily early-warning signs
- social cue patterns
- sensory cue patterns
- authority/power cue patterns
- reaction modes

### Phase 3: Reaction versus explanation

Goal: separate immediate state from later narrative.

Output:

- immediate reaction records
- delayed explanation records
- reasoning-delay estimates
- confidence labels
- alternative explanations

### Phase 4: Anchors and recovery

Goal: identify stabilising infrastructure.

Output:

- anchor records
- stabilising actions
- recovery patterns
- anchor-loss failure modes

### Phase 5: Friction, stakes, and social power

Goal: identify hidden forces that change execution.

Output:

- executive-friction records
- action-gap records
- private stakes
- authority/gatekeeper patterns
- social threat maps

### Phase 6: Memory, contradiction, absence, and tacit skill

Goal: preserve the non-obvious signal.

Output:

- memory activation records
- contradiction records
- absence/non-event records
- tacit-skill records
- disputed or uncertain patterns

### Phase 7: Falsification and hostile audit

Goal: prevent self-sealing narratives.

Output:

- falsification paths
- contradictory evidence candidates
- hostile-audit notes
- claims that must not become durable without records

### Phase 8: Compression and runtime packet extraction

Goal: identify what must survive context limits.

Output:

- high-signal facts
- must-preserve contradictions
- must-preserve uncertainties
- runtime packet candidates
- update-gate candidates

### Phase 9: Integrity pass

Goal: decide what can become durable HSM material.

For each significant claim, decide:

```text
reject
keep as transient output
store as hypothesis
store as low-confidence observation
store as durable claim
store as preference update
store as state update
store as open uncertainty
```

## General questioning rules

Use these after most answers:

```text
How do you know that?

Is that direct memory, record-backed fact, someone else's observation, later explanation, or current interpretation?

What would contradict it?

Was that true at the time, or is it something you understood later?

What did your body do before you had words for it?

What did other people see?

What is the clean story, and what does the clean story leave out?
```

Do not ask all of them every time. Use them as calibration checks.

## Minimal five-question version

Use this when time or emotional bandwidth is limited.

```text
1. What changes your state before you can explain it?

2. What restores you when you are destabilised?

3. What do you repeatedly do that contradicts what you say about yourself?

4. What absences, silences, or non-events shaped you?

5. For each explanation you give, what is evidence, what is inference, and what would falsify it?
```

## Integrity pass after deposition

After the deposition, perform an integrity pass.

For each extracted item, ask:

1. Is this direct evidence, self-report, third-party observation, later explanation, inference, or hypothesis?
2. What source supports it?
3. What source contradicts it?
4. Is the date known?
5. Is the context known?
6. Is the confidence appropriate?
7. Is it sensitive?
8. Is it a durable claim or an open uncertainty?
9. Was the interviewer leading the subject?
10. Did the model agree too easily?
11. Did emotional coherence get mistaken for evidence?
12. Did compression erase uncertainty?

## Updating HSM state

Do not write deposition output directly into durable subject state.

Use the update gate:

```text
raw answer
  -> extracted candidate
  -> claim classification
  -> source/provenance check
  -> contradiction check
  -> confidence assignment
  -> sensitivity label
  -> durable state, hypothesis, transient note, or rejection
```

Possible update classes:

```text
reject
keep_as_transient_output
store_as_hypothesis
store_as_low_confidence_observation
store_as_durable_claim
store_as_preference_update
store_as_state_update
store_as_open_uncertainty
```

## Practical interviewer notes

Good interviewer behaviour:

- ask slowly
- tolerate silence
- capture uncertainty
- ask for examples
- ask for counterexamples
- ask how the subject knows
- ask what would falsify it
- let contradiction stand
- stop before exhaustion destroys signal quality

Bad interviewer behaviour:

- chasing trauma detail when structure is enough
- treating distress as proof
- forcing coherence
- rewarding only articulate answers
- using the subject's explanation as the final truth
- converting everything into diagnosis
- letting the model summarize away uncertainty

## Language-model self-warning

A language model running this protocol should remember:

```text
I do not have a body.
I do not have lived continuity.
I do not experience stakes.
I do not experience involuntary memory.
I do not know what pain, hunger, fatigue, shame, or sensory overload are from the inside.
I can generate plausible explanations too easily.
I must ask for state, evidence, uncertainty, and falsification.
```

The model's job is not to explain the human into existence.

The model's job is to help extract, structure, classify, and preserve evidence-backed state.

## Final operating line

The deposition is successful when it produces useful, inspectable state without pretending uncertainty disappeared.

Good result:

```text
We know these patterns.
We have evidence for these claims.
These are plausible hypotheses.
These contradictions must remain visible.
These claims are emotionally coherent but not yet proven.
These missing records matter.
This is what a runtime packet needs for the next task.
```

Bad result:

```text
We made a clean story, so now we understand the person.
```
